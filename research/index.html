Creatine monohydrate

Performance Strong
Across 69 randomized trials, adding creatine to resistance training meant about 5.6 kg more on the squat, 1.4 kg more on the bench press, a higher vertical jump and more peak power.[7]Kazeminasab 2025 · 1,937 participants
What the research shows
  • A meta-analysis of 60 studies and 1,297 people found creatine improved squat, leg press and quadriceps strength, with about 8% more weight on the maximal squat.[6]
  • The 2025 analysis also found gains of about 1.5 cm in vertical jump and about 48 W in Wingate peak power compared with placebo.[7]
  • The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) concluded that creatine raises the creatine stored in muscle, which may explain improvements in high-intensity performance and in training adaptations.[1]
  • Once muscle stores are full, the ISSN says 3 to 5 g a day maintains them. Supplementation was found safe and well tolerated, with studies running up to 30 g a day for 5 years.[1]
Why it is in QUBE

Creatine is QUBE's performance anchor. It is stored in muscle as phosphocreatine, a fast fuel reserve your body leans on during sprints, jumps and heavy sets, and 5 g is the daily amount the ISSN lists for keeping stores topped up.[1]

Good to know. Creatine builds up in muscle with daily use. The 2025 review found the gains clearest in younger adults and men, and it calls for more studies in women.[7]
2g

Beta-alanine

Performance Well supported
In trained sprinters, four weeks of beta-alanine raised muscle carnosine by up to 47%, and torque held up better in the later bouts of repeated hard efforts.[9]Derave 2007 · 4.8 g a day
What the research shows
  • A meta-analysis of 40 studies and 1,461 participants found a significant overall benefit. For efforts lasting 0.5 to 10 minutes, the effect on exercise capacity (how long you can keep going) was moderate, at about 0.5 standard deviations.[8]
  • The ISSN position stand reports that 4 to 6 g a day for at least two to four weeks raises muscle carnosine and improves exercise performance, with the clearest gains in efforts lasting about one to four minutes.[2]
  • The ISSN also notes beta-alanine reduces neuromuscular fatigue, particularly in older people.[2]
Why it is in QUBE

Beta-alanine is the building block your body uses to make carnosine, a compound in muscle that helps buffer the acid that builds during hard efforts. It pairs with creatine in QUBE's performance pillar: creatine for power, beta-alanine for holding it.[2]

Good to know. Carnosine builds over weeks of daily use. The tingling some people feel is the only reported side effect, and smaller divided doses (about 1.6 g) reduce it.[2]

Tart cherry extract

Recovery Supported
Pooling 14 studies, tart cherry produced a moderate benefit for recovery of strength and a large one for recovery of jump height, with lower CRP and IL-6.[4]Meta-analysis · 2021
What the research shows
  • The 2021 meta-analysis found moderate benefits for recovery of muscular strength and power, a large effect on jump height, a small effect on sprint time and a small reduction in muscle soreness.[4]
  • In a placebo-controlled study of 20 marathon runners, strength recovered significantly faster with tart cherry juice. Inflammation markers IL-6, CRP and uric acid were lower, and total antioxidant status was about 10% higher.[10]
  • A systematic review of 25 studies found benefits are more likely when supplementation starts some days before hard exercise and continues for some days after, for at least 8 to 10 days in total.[11]
  • A 2026 review suggests tart cherry as a tool for periods of high recovery demand, such as congested schedules, short turnarounds and travel.[3]
Why it is in QUBE

Tart cherries are rich in anthocyanins, the pigments that give them their deep red color. Their antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties are why they are studied for recovery, and why tart cherry anchors QUBE's recovery pillar.[3]

Good to know. Tart cherry works as a daily habit. The studies loaded it for several days around hard training, and the research links the best results to at least 1,000 mg a day of total phenolic compounds.[11]
500mg

Curcumin

Inflammation · Recovery Promising
Pooling 10 randomized trials, curcumin lowered muscle damage and soreness after hard exercise and improved strength and range of motion.[12]Beba 2022 · 10 randomized trials
What the research shows
  • The 2022 meta-analysis found curcumin reduced creatine kinase (a marker of muscle damage) by about 66 IU/L, reduced muscle soreness and TNF-alpha (an inflammation marker), and improved range of motion by about 6.5 degrees.[12]
  • A 2024 systematic review of 11 studies in people who exercise regularly concluded that curcumin has significant potential to relieve muscle symptoms, especially delayed-onset muscle soreness, and appears to lower inflammation markers and raise antioxidant levels. Post-exercise intake appeared more beneficial.[13]
  • A 2020 review identifies reduced inflammatory response as the most probable mechanism and describes typical daily doses of 150 to 1,500 mg.[14]
Why it is in QUBE

Curcumin is the main active compound in turmeric. It is QUBE's inflammation pillar, studied for the muscle soreness and inflammation that follow hard training.[13]

Good to know. The research focuses on eccentric exercise, where muscles lengthen under load and soreness is greatest.[13]

Electrolytes

Hydration Foundational
Magnesium takes part in energy metabolism and normal muscle function, and the research finds the body's need for it rises as activity level goes up.[5]Zhang 2017 · review of animal and human studies
What the research shows
  • A 2017 review describes magnesium as a cofactor in energy metabolism and glycolysis, and reports that the need for it increases with physical activity.[5]
  • Cross-sectional studies linked magnesium status with grip strength, leg power, knee extension torque and jumping performance, and intervention studies reported improvements in indices such as quadriceps torque.[5]
  • The Recommended Dietary Allowance for magnesium is 400 to 420 mg for men and 310 to 320 mg for women.[5]
Why it is in QUBE

Sodium, potassium and magnesium are minerals your body uses for fluid balance and for the electrical signals that run your muscles and nerves, and you lose some in sweat. QUBE puts them back in a balanced blend as the hydration pillar.
