Creatine for female athletes: what research shows

If you are a woman who plays a sport, you have probably been told creatine is "for guys." The research tells a more careful story. Creatine for female athletes has less evidence behind it than creatine for men, and it is worth knowing where that evidence is solid and where it is thin.

Does creatine work for female athletes?

Possibly, but the evidence is thinner than in men. A 2025 systematic review of 27 studies in active females found that, compared with placebo, only a minority of studies reported improvements in strength and power, anaerobic or aerobic outcomes.[1]

Here is how that review worked. Tam and colleagues searched five databases through July 2024 and screened 10,563 records to find 27 eligible studies. Participants ranged from recreationally active to elite. The authors noted that creatine's performance benefits have mostly been shown in men, which is the gap this review set out to look at.[1]

A 2021 narrative review by Smith-Ryan and colleagues takes a more optimistic view. It reports benefits for strength and exercise performance in pre-menopausal women, though it is a review of the literature rather than a pooled analysis.[3] Put the two together and the honest read is this: creatine may help some women in some situations, and the research is still catching up.

Is creatine safe for women?

The best pooled evidence says yes for healthy women. A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of female-only studies found no deaths or serious adverse outcomes, and no significant differences in total adverse events, gastrointestinal events or weight gain.[2]

Davies-Tuck and colleagues found 656 studies where creatine was the main intervention, and only 58 (9%) included only female participants. Of those, 29 monitored adverse outcomes, covering 951 participants. Kidney and liver function measures also showed no statistically significant differences.[2] The authors called for better reporting of adverse outcomes in future female studies, which is a fair reminder that the data pool is still small.

The ISSN position stand reached a similar conclusion for healthy people generally: supplementation up to 30 g a day for 5 years was safe and well tolerated.[4] If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or take medication, check with a doctor or athletic trainer first.

Why might women respond differently to creatine?

One reason is baseline stores. Smith-Ryan and colleagues report that females have 70 to 80% lower endogenous creatine stores than males, and argue that hormonal changes across life stages may affect how creatine is used.[3]

That is a hypothesis from a narrative review, not a proven rule, and it does not tell you what you will personally notice. It is a reason researchers want more female-specific trials, which is exactly the gap the 2025 review describes.[1]

How much creatine should female athletes take?

The sources reviewed here do not set a separate dose for women. The ISSN describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintaining muscle creatine in healthy people, with higher short-term loading doses as an option.[4]

Loading is a speed option, not a requirement. Our post on creatine loading covers the trade-offs, including the water weight that can come with a big short-term dose. Dose per body weight in women is not something the sources I opened settle, so I am not going to guess at it here.

The bottom line on creatine for female athletes

Creatine for female athletes is safe in the research that exists, but the proof that it improves performance is thinner than the proof in men. That is not a reason to skip it or to take it blindly. It is a reason to read the label, know the dose and be honest about what the studies do and do not show.

FAQ

Is creatine safe for female athletes?

A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis of female-only trials that monitored adverse outcomes (29 studies, 951 participants) reported no deaths or serious adverse outcomes and no significant differences in total adverse events, gastrointestinal events or weight gain. Check with a doctor or athletic trainer first if you have a medical condition or take medication.

Does creatine work as well for women as for men?

That is not settled. A 2025 systematic review of 27 studies in active females found that, compared with placebo, a minority of studies reported improvements in strength, power, anaerobic or aerobic outcomes, and noted that most of the creatine evidence comes from men.

Do female athletes need a different creatine dose?

The sources reviewed here do not establish a separate dose for women. The ISSN describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintaining muscle stores in healthy people generally.

Do women need to load creatine?

The sources reviewed here do not say women need a loading phase. Loading is a speed option, and a steady daily dose reaches similar stores over a few weeks. See our post on creatine loading for the details.
