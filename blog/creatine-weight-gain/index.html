If you have started creatine and the scale moved, you are not imagining it. Creatine weight gain is one of the most common questions athletes ask, and the research gives a clearer answer than most gym talk does. Here is what the studies measured, what they cannot tell you, and what to do with it.

Does creatine make you gain weight?

Usually a little, yes. A 2024 meta-analysis of 143 studies found creatine supplementation increased body mass by about 0.86 kg (roughly 1.9 lb) compared with control groups. The effect is small, and it was larger when people trained with resistance exercise or used a maintenance dose.[1]

Creatine monohydrate is a compound stored in muscle as phosphocreatine. It is not a stimulant and it is not a mass gainer. If you want the full background, start with our post on creatine for athletes.

How much creatine weight gain should you expect?

The average was under 1 kg in that meta-analysis, which pooled 143 studies across many populations and protocols. Individual results vary, and the authors describe the overall effect as small.[1]

Those are group averages from Pashayee-Khamene et al. They do not predict what the scale will do for you.

Does creatine weight gain mean water or muscle?

Both can be involved. Fat-free mass is everything in your body that is not fat, including water and muscle, so that number alone cannot split the two. The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) authors describe the early water gain as primarily an increase in intracellular (inside the cell) volume over the short term.[2]

The same review reports mixed findings. One trial it cites found total body water and intracellular water rose more with creatine than with placebo over 8 weeks of resistance training, while extracellular water (the fluid outside cells) rose in both groups. Another trial it describes found total body water increased with no change in intracellular or extracellular volume.[2]

In a randomized trial of 30 moderately active women, 20 g a day for 5 days raised measured body water in one phase of the menstrual cycle, using bioelectrical impedance spectroscopy, while body mass did not change meaningfully.[3] It is a small study, so treat it as one data point. Our post on whether creatine dehydrates you covers the related hydration question.

Does creatine weight gain happen without loading?

The big early jumps are linked to loading. The ISSN review ties 20 g a day for six days to water retention, and it also describes trials where roughly 5 g a day showed no significant change in body water measures.[2] Evidence is mixed, so expect a gradual change rather than a guarantee of none.

If you are deciding how to start, read creatine loading: do you need to load creatine? and when to take creatine. If you are a woman wondering how this plays out, see creatine for female athletes.

What if you have a weigh-in or a weight limit?

Plan for a small change and talk to your coach or athletic trainer before you start, not the week of a weigh-in. A shift of a pound or two in the meta-analysis average is meaningful if you compete in a weight class, and meaningless if you do not.[1]

FAQ

Does creatine make you gain weight?

Usually a little. A 2024 meta-analysis of 143 studies found creatine users gained about 0.86 kg more body mass than comparison groups, and the gain is larger with resistance training or a maintenance dose.

Is creatine weight gain just water?

Not only water. Fat-free mass rose by about 0.82 kg in the same meta-analysis, and an ISSN review describes early water gains as mainly inside muscle cells. Scale weight alone cannot separate water from muscle.

Does creatine make you look puffy or bloated?

The research reviewed here measured body water and body mass, not how people look or feel. The ISSN review reports the water increase is primarily inside muscle cells over the short term, which is different from fluid under the skin.

Does 5 g of creatine a day cause weight gain without loading?

The studies show mixed results on water, and the biggest early jumps are tied to high-dose loading phases. A steady daily dose is a slower build. Each QUBE has 5 g of creatine monohydrate and no loading phase.
