Creatine loading: do you need to load creatine?

If you have ever looked up how to start creatine, you have seen the loading phase: a week of big doses before the "real" routine begins. Creatine loading is real, but is it necessary? Here is what the research says, in plain English.

What is creatine loading?

Creatine loading means taking a high dose of creatine monohydrate for about a week to fill your muscles' creatine stores quickly, then dropping to a smaller daily amount. The ISSN describes about 5 g four times a day (roughly 0.3 g per kg of body weight) for 5 to 7 days, then 3 to 5 g a day.[3]

The idea is simple. Creatine works by raising what is stored in muscle, so loading is a shortcut to a full tank. If you want the basics first, start with our post on creatine for athletes.

Do you need to load creatine?

No. Loading raises muscle creatine faster, but it is not the only route. In a classic muscle biopsy study, 3 g a day for 28 days raised muscle creatine by about as much as 20 g a day for 6 days, and the two groups ended up at a similar level.[1]

Here is how that study worked. Hultman and colleagues gave 31 healthy, regularly exercising men (not highly trained) different creatine regimens and took muscle samples from the thigh. Total muscle creatine rose by roughly 23 mmol/kg dry mass after the 6-day high-dose phase. On 3 g a day, it climbed by roughly 20 mmol/kg dry mass by day 28. The authors concluded the lower dose works just as well in the long run, while the high dose is quicker.[1]

A 2021 expert review from the same research community says it plainly: you do not have to load creatine. It recommends about 3 to 5 g a day for a minimum of four weeks to reach similar saturation, and notes that skipping the load delays maximum storage.[2]

When does creatine loading make sense?

Creatine loading makes sense mainly when you are short on time and want full stores quickly. The 2021 review says loading may be advised for short-term goals.[2] If you are starting a month or more before your season, the steady route reaches the same place.

Think of it like this: loading buys speed, and you pay for it with bigger doses and a few side effects, covered next.

Does creatine loading cause water weight or stomach problems?

It can. Creatine loading typically comes with a 1 to 3 kg increase in body mass, mostly from water retention, which the review attributes primarily to extra water inside muscle cells over the short term.[2] Single doses above 10 g may also cause stomach distress, including diarrhea.[2]

If you track your weight for a weigh-in, a position or a cut, that scale jump is worth planning around. On the safety side, the ISSN position stand concluded that short and long term creatine supplementation, up to 30 g a day for 5 years, is safe and well tolerated in healthy individuals.[3] Check with your doctor or athletic trainer first if you have a medical condition or take medication.

The bottom line on creatine loading

Creatine loading is a speed option, not a requirement. A steady 3 to 5 g a day gets muscle to a similar level in about a month, with less water weight and a gentler start. FAQ

Do you need to load creatine?

No. Loading fills muscle creatine faster, but a steady 3 to 5 g a day gets muscle to a similar level over about four weeks. The ISSN-affiliated review by Antonio and colleagues says you do not have to load creatine.

How long does it take to saturate muscle without loading?

In Hultman and colleagues' 1996 study, 3 g a day for 28 days raised muscle creatine by about the same amount as 6 days at 20 g a day. Antonio and colleagues suggest 3 to 5 g a day for at least four weeks.
