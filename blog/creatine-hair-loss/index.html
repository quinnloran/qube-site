Creatine and hair loss: what the research shows

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Oct 29, 2026Updated Oct 29, 2026Ingredients5 minute read

Key takeaways

  • The creatine hair loss worry traces back to a 2009 study of 20 rugby players that found higher DHT, a hormone made from testosterone.
  • A 2025 randomized trial gave men 5 g a day for 12 weeks and found no difference from placebo in DHT or in hair density and thickness measures.
  • Neither study answers everything. The trial covered 38 men over 12 weeks, so women and longer use are not tested.
  • Each QUBE has 5 g of creatine monohydrate, taken once a day in the morning.

If you lift or play a sport, you have probably heard that creatine makes you go bald. The creatine hair loss claim spreads fast, so here is where it came from and what the newest trial found. I want to be clear about what these studies measured and what they did not.

Does creatine cause hair loss?

No study I opened shows that creatine causes hair loss. A 2025 randomized controlled trial measured hormones and hair follicles directly and found no difference between creatine and placebo. An older 2009 study did find higher DHT, but it did not report hair outcomes in its abstract.[1][2]

That is a narrow answer on purpose. "No evidence it causes hair loss" is not the same as "proven impossible for everyone," and I will point out the gaps below.

What did the 2009 study find about creatine and DHT?

It found that DHT rose 56% after a 7 day creatine loading phase in college-aged rugby players. DHT was still 40% above baseline after 14 days at 5 g a day, while testosterone did not change significantly.[2]

DHT (dihydrotestosterone) is a hormone made from testosterone, and it is the reason creatine got linked to hair loss. Here is what the study actually was:

  • 20 male rugby players in a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design.
  • A 25 g a day loading phase for 7 days, then 5 g a day for 14 days.
  • The authors suggested creatine may increase the conversion of testosterone to DHT and called for more research on long-term safety.

Notice what is missing. The abstract reports hormone levels, not hair. A hormone change is a reason to ask the question, not an answer to it. If you want the loading details, see our post on creatine loading.

What did the 2025 trial find about creatine and hair loss?

Lak and colleagues randomized 45 resistance-trained men to 5 g a day of creatine monohydrate or placebo for 12 weeks. Of the 38 who finished, there were no group-by-time differences in DHT, the DHT to testosterone ratio or hair measures.[1]

This trial went after the exact question the 2009 study raised. The researchers measured total and free testosterone and DHT from blood. They assessed hair follicles with a Trichogram test and the FotoFinder system, which looked at hair density, follicular unit count and cumulative hair thickness. The authors describe their results as strong evidence against the claim that creatine contributes to hair loss, and say it is the first study to directly assess hair follicle health after creatine supplementation.[1]

What is still unknown about creatine and hair loss?

Quite a bit. The 2025 trial covered men aged 18 to 40 over 12 weeks at 5 g a day, so it does not test women, older adults, loading doses or years of use. It also looked at the group average, not every individual.

Those limits are mine, drawn from what the trial enrolled, not claims from the authors. For broad safety context, the ISSN position stand concluded that creatine supplementation up to 30 g a day for 5 years was safe and well tolerated in healthy people.[3] That statement is about general safety, not hair specifically. If you want the bigger picture on creatine, start with creatine for athletes.

The bottom line on creatine and hair loss

The creatine hair loss claim started with a hormone change in one small study. The best direct test since then, a 12 week randomized trial, did not find a difference in DHT or hair measures. That is reassuring, but it is one trial in one group. If hair loss worries you, talk with a doctor, dermatologist or athletic trainer about your own situation.

FAQ

Does creatine cause hair loss?

No study I opened shows that it does. A 2025 randomized trial of 38 men who finished 12 weeks of 5 g a day found no differences from placebo in DHT or in hair density and thickness measures. One 2009 study did find higher DHT, but it did not report hair outcomes in its abstract.

Does creatine raise DHT?

In one 2009 study of 20 male rugby players, DHT rose 56% after a 7 day loading phase and stayed 40% above baseline after 14 days at 5 g a day. A 2025 trial at 5 g a day for 12 weeks found no group difference in DHT versus placebo.

Is the 2025 hair loss trial the final word?

No. It studied 45 resistance-trained men aged 18 to 40 for 12 weeks, so it does not cover women, other ages or longer use. It is the first study I found that measured hair follicles directly.

Should I stop taking creatine if I am worried about hair loss?

That is a personal call. If hair loss is a concern, talk with a doctor, dermatologist or athletic trainer who knows your history and can look at your own situation.
