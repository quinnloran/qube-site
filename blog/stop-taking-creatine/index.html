What happens when you stop taking creatine?

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Nov 26, 2026Updated Nov 26, 2026Ingredients5 minute read

  • The ISSN reports no evidence that stores fall below baseline afterward.[2]

Winter break, a trip home, an empty tub you forgot to replace. Sooner or later a lot of us stop taking creatine for a stretch, then wonder if we just threw away months of work. Here is what the research I could check actually shows, and where it goes quiet.

What happens when you stop taking creatine?

When you stop taking creatine, muscle creatine stores slowly return to where they started. Supplementing raises the amount of creatine held in muscle. Without the extra daily supply, that raised level declines over several weeks. It is a gradual drift, not a crash the next morning.[1][2]

Muscle creatine stores are the pool of creatine (including phosphocreatine) your muscles draw on during short, hard efforts like sprints, jumps and heavy sets. New to this? Start with what creatine does for athletes.

How long does creatine stay after you stop taking it?

The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) says it generally takes 4 to 6 weeks for elevated creatine stores to return to baseline.[2] A classic 1996 muscle biopsy study lines up: after a short loading phase, muscle creatine was no different from its starting level 30 days after stopping.[1]

In that study, Hultman and colleagues worked with 31 healthy men who exercised regularly but were not highly trained. They took thigh muscle samples across different creatine routines.[1]

Hultman 1996: what happened to muscle creatine
Routine studiedResult in muscle
20 g a day for 6 daysTotal creatine rose about 20%[1]
Load, then 2 g a day for about a monthThe raised level was maintained[1]
Load, then nothingGradual decline, back to the starting level 30 days after stopping[1]
3 g a day for 28 daysA similar 20% rise, more slowly[1]

The fine print matters. The stop-and-watch group was 6 men who had taken creatine for only 6 days. The study did not include women, and it did not test stopping after months of daily use.[1]

Does your body stop making its own creatine?

Your body makes some creatine on its own. The ISSN reports no evidence that muscle creatine falls below baseline after people stop supplementing, and concludes that long-term suppression of your own production does not appear to occur.[2]

The 1996 authors reached a similar idea from urine measurements, writing that the body's own production may not be inhibited. They did not measure production directly, so treat that as a clue rather than proof.[1]

Will you lose strength or weight if you stop taking creatine?

I cannot give you a verified number. Neither the 1996 study nor the ISSN's comments on stopping report what happens to strength, muscle or body weight afterward. They tell you what happens to stored creatine, which is a different measurement.[1][2]

Here is what is known on the way up. The ISSN says weight gain is the only side effect of creatine consistently reported in the research.[2] That does not tell you how fast, or how much, the scale changes once you quit.

How should you restart creatine after a break?

You can simply resume a steady daily dose. In the 1996 study, 3 g a day for 28 days raised muscle creatine about as much as a 6-day high-dose load, just more slowly.[1] The ISSN describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintaining elevated stores.[2]

  • Short on time before the season? Read the trade-offs of creatine loading first.
  • Keep forgetting? Tie it to something you already do. More on that in when to take creatine.
  • Stopped because of a health concern? Talk with a doctor or athletic trainer before you start again.

FAQ

How long does creatine stay in your system after you stop?

The ISSN position stand says it generally takes 4 to 6 weeks for elevated muscle creatine stores to return to baseline. In a 1996 study of men who loaded for 6 days, muscle creatine was no different from its starting value 30 days after they stopped.

Do you lose strength when you stop taking creatine?

The two sources behind this post do not report strength after stopping, so I will not give you a number. What they show is that muscle creatine stores drift back toward baseline over several weeks.

Does your body stop making creatine if you supplement?

The ISSN position stand says there is no evidence that muscle creatine falls below baseline after stopping, so long-term suppression of your own creatine production does not appear to occur.

Do you need to cycle off creatine?

The ISSN sections I checked do not call for cycling creatine. The position stand describes 3 to 5 g a day to maintain elevated stores. Ask a doctor first if you have a medical condition or take medication.
