Creatine for basketball players: what research shows

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Nov 7, 2026Updated Nov 7, 2026Performance4 minute read

Key takeaways

  • Creatine has the strongest evidence of any supplement for short, hard efforts like jumps, sprints and heavy sets.
  • A 2025 meta-analysis of 69 trials found about 1.5 cm more vertical jump with creatine plus training.
  • Most studies are not basketball-only, so the evidence is indirect, and it is thinner for women.
  • The research is about daily use, not a pre-game dose.

I play D-III basketball at Johns Hopkins, so this is the question I get most. Here is what the research says about creatine for basketball players, and what nobody has proven yet.

Why do basketball players take creatine?

Basketball is repeated short, hard efforts: jumps, sprints, cuts and screens. Creatine is stored in muscle as phosphocreatine, a fast fuel reserve for exactly those efforts. The ISSN concluded that raising muscle creatine may explain gains in high-intensity exercise performance and training adaptations.[1]

That is the logic. Next question: does it show up in the numbers?

Does creatine help basketball players jump and get stronger?

On average, yes, a little. A 2025 meta-analysis of 69 trials found creatine plus training added about 1.5 cm (roughly 0.6 inches) to vertical jump versus placebo. An earlier meta-analysis found about 8% more weight on the maximal squat.[2][3]

Results varied a lot between studies. The jump gain was significant in males and trained people but not in the female subgroup, where the authors noted few studies exist.[2] Read the full breakdown in supplements for vertical jump.

How strong is the evidence for basketball specifically?

It is indirect. These meta-analyses pool athletes and lifters across many sports, not only basketball players. Creatine for basketball players is a reasonable application of that research, but this post does not cite a basketball-only trial, and QUBE itself has not been studied in basketball.

Is creatine a pre-game supplement?

No. Creatine works by building up in muscle with daily use, so the research is about consistency, not a dose before tip-off. QUBE follows that logic: one QUBE mixed into 12 to 16 oz of water as part of your morning routine.

Where does beta-alanine fit for basketball?

Beta-alanine is a performance ingredient, not a recovery one. It helps buffer acid in hard efforts, and its best-supported role is in efforts lasting about 1 to 4 minutes.[4] Basketball is mostly shorter bursts, so creatine is the anchor.

FAQ

Is creatine good for basketball players?

Creatine has the best evidence of any supplement for short, hard efforts, which is most of basketball. The ISSN concluded it may support high-intensity performance and training adaptations. Basketball-only trials are limited, so most of the evidence comes from athletes and lifters in general.

Will creatine make me jump higher?

A 2025 meta-analysis of 69 trials found creatine plus training added about 1.5 cm (roughly 0.6 inches) to vertical jump versus placebo. Results varied a lot between studies, so treat it as a small average gain, not a promise.

Should basketball players take creatine in season?

The research on creatine is about daily use building up in muscle over time, not a pre-game dose. That is why QUBE is a daily powder you mix into water in the morning. Check with your athletic trainer or doctor about your own situation.
