Does creatine hurt your kidneys? What research shows

Does creatine hurt your kidneys? It is the question I hear most from teammates who are thinking about starting. Here is what the research says, what it does not say, and how to think about your own situation.

Does creatine hurt your kidneys?

In the research reviewed here, creatine did not damage kidneys in healthy people. A 2025 meta-analysis found a small rise in serum creatinine but no significant difference in glomerular filtration rate (GFR), the standard measure of how well the kidneys filter blood.[1]

That does not mean every person is the same. People with existing kidney problems were not the focus of this post, and they should talk with a doctor first.

What does the 2025 meta-analysis say about creatine and kidney function?

Researchers pooled studies from 2000 to March 2025. The review included 21 studies, and 12 had data on serum creatinine (177 people taking creatine, 263 controls). Creatine was linked to a small rise in creatinine, with no significant GFR difference.[1]

The timing mattered. The rise was significant at one week or less, not significant from one to twelve weeks, and significant again beyond twelve weeks. The authors described the change as modest and transient, and said it reflects metabolic turnover rather than kidney impairment.[1]

Why does creatine raise creatinine on a blood test?

Creatinine is a by-product of creatine metabolism, so more creatine in the body can nudge the number up. The meta-analysis authors attributed the rise to turnover, not to harm, because GFR did not change.[1]

Practical tip: doctors often use creatinine to check kidney function. If you take creatine, tell your doctor or athletic trainer before bloodwork so they can read the result in context.

What does the ISSN say about long-term creatine safety?

The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand states that short and long-term supplementation, up to 30 g a day for 5 years, is safe and well tolerated in healthy people and in patient groups from infants to the elderly.[2]

A later ISSN review of common creatine questions lists kidney function among the topics it covers, and notes creatine is generally well tolerated at the commonly recommended 3 to 5 g a day (or about 0.1 g per kg of body mass).[4]

What about people with existing health conditions?

One randomized, placebo-controlled trial gave creatine or placebo for 12 weeks to adults with type 2 diabetes who also did exercise training. Kidney filtration measured by 51Cr-EDTA clearance did not differ between groups.[3]

That is one trial in one group, so it is not a green light for everyone. If you have a kidney or other health condition, a doctor should make the call.

FAQ

Does creatine hurt your kidneys?

In healthy people, the research reviewed here does not show kidney damage. A 2025 meta-analysis found a small rise in serum creatinine but no significant change in glomerular filtration rate (GFR). Talk with a doctor first if you have a kidney condition.

Why does creatine raise creatinine on blood tests?

Creatinine is a by-product of creatine metabolism. The authors of the 2025 meta-analysis attributed the modest rise to metabolic turnover rather than kidney impairment. Tell your doctor you take creatine before bloodwork.

How much creatine has been studied for safety?

The ISSN position stand states that supplementation up to 30 g a day for 5 years was safe and well tolerated in healthy people and patient groups. Common recommended doses are about 3 to 5 g a day.

Should I check with a doctor before taking creatine?

Yes if you have a kidney condition, take medication or have any health concern. A doctor or athletic trainer can look at your situation. This post is not medical advice.
