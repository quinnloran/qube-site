Potassium for athletes: what sweat tests show

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Nov 25, 2026Updated Nov 25, 2026Hydration4 minute read

Key takeaways

  • Final sweat potassium is about 2 to 8 mmol/L, similar to blood plasma, according to a 2017 review.[1]
  • The same review puts whole-body sweat sodium at roughly 10 to 70 mmol/L, so sweat carries a much lower concentration of potassium than sodium.[1]
  • A 2019 review lists no effect of dehydration and minimal or no effect of exercise intensity on sweat potassium.[2]
  • These sweat studies do not say how much potassium to take or whether it prevents cramps. I left those claims out.

Potassium for athletes gets far less attention than sodium, so I went looking for what sweat studies actually measured. The short version: sweat carries a much lower concentration of potassium than sodium, and that concentration does not swing much with how hard you train.

What is potassium and why do athletes care?

Potassium is an electrolyte, a mineral that carries an electrical charge in body fluids. Along with sodium and magnesium, it helps with fluid balance and with the signals that run muscles and nerves, which is why it comes up in any talk about hydration for athletes.

Most hydration talk is about sodium, because sodium is the mineral researchers track most closely in sweat.[1] Potassium is the quieter partner, and the numbers are worth a look.

How much potassium do athletes lose in sweat?

A 2017 review reports that final sweat potassium concentration is similar to blood plasma, with a slightly broader range of about 2 to 8 mmol/L. The same review says sweat potassium loss has been reported to be indirectly related to sweat flow rate, but the underlying mechanism is unclear.[1]

The review's author, Lindsay Baker, also uses potassium as a quality check. If a sweat sample reads far above the normal range (above about 10 mmol/L), the sample may have been contaminated or have evaporated, so researchers treat it with suspicion.[1]

How does sweat potassium compare with sweat sodium?

Sweat potassium concentration is much lower than sweat sodium concentration in the same review. Whole-body sweat sodium is estimated at roughly 10 to 70 mmol/L, against about 2 to 8 mmol/L for potassium. Sweating rate typically runs about 0.5 to 2.0 L/h.[1]

Typical ranges reported in a 2017 review of athletes
MeasureReported range
Whole-body sweating rateAbout 0.5 to 2.0 L/h
Whole-body sweat sodium (estimated)About 10 to 70 mmol/L
Final sweat potassiumAbout 2 to 8 mmol/L

For sodium, the abstract of a 2011 review adds that active athletes in hot weather can lose 3,500 to 7,000 mg of sodium per day (and 4 to 10 L of water).[3] I did not find a matching daily potassium figure in the sources I could open, so I am not going to guess one.

Does sweat potassium change with intensity, dehydration or heat?

Mostly no. A 2019 review of sweat physiology lists no effect of dehydration on sweat potassium, minimal or no effect of exercise intensity, no effect of drinking water, and no change with heat acclimation.[2] Sweat sodium is the more variable mineral.[1]

That is useful context. These reviews describe sodium as the mineral that varies most between athletes, so that is where the planning conversation usually starts. If you want the sodium side, read electrolytes for athletes.

What do these studies not tell athletes about potassium?

They do not tell you how much potassium to take, whether potassium supplements improve performance, whether potassium prevents cramps, or how it affects rehydration after practice. They describe what is in sweat, not what to do about it.

I could not open and read research on those questions this time, so I left them out instead of guessing. For the one cramp-related mineral I have read about, see magnesium for muscle cramps. Questions about cramps or your own intake belong with a doctor or athletic trainer.

FAQ

How much potassium do athletes lose in sweat?

A 2017 review reports that final sweat potassium is similar to blood plasma, with a range of about 2 to 8 mmol/L. That is a low concentration next to sodium, which the same review puts at roughly 10 to 70 mmol/L for whole-body sweat.

Does sweat potassium change when you are dehydrated?

A 2019 review of sweat physiology lists no effect of dehydration on sweat potassium concentration. It also lists minimal or no effect of exercise intensity, and no effect of drinking water. Sweat sodium is more variable.

Do potassium supplements prevent cramps?

The sources reviewed for this post do not answer that question, so I am not making that claim. For the cramp research I did read, see my post on magnesium for muscle cramps, and ask a doctor or athletic trainer about individual cramp problems.
