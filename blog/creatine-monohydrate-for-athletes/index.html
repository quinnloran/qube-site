Creatine is one of the first things athletes hear about, and one of the most misunderstood. Here is a plain read on what the research says about creatine monohydrate, what it does not say, and why it is in every QUBE.

What is creatine monohydrate?

Creatine is a compound your body makes and also gets from food. Most of it is stored in muscle as phosphocreatine. Supplementing raises those stores, and monohydrate is the form QUBE uses.

Phosphocreatine is a quick-access fuel reserve. During short, hard efforts like a sprint, a jump or a heavy set, your muscles burn through energy fast, and phosphocreatine helps them keep up.

What does creatine do for athletes?

Creatine raises the amount stored in muscle. The ISSN concluded that this may explain gains in high-intensity exercise performance and in training adaptations. In plain terms, it supports short, hard efforts and the work you do to get better at them.[1]

That is the core of the case for creatine. It is not a stimulant and it does not give you a buzz. It works by raising the creatine stored in your muscles.

Is creatine safe?

For healthy people, the ISSN found it safe and well tolerated, in both short-term and long-term use. Daily doses much higher than 5 g were studied for years without safety concerns.[1]

As with any supplement, talk with your doctor or athletic trainer first if you have a medical condition or take medication.

What is not proven yet?

The ISSN lists recovery, injury prevention and several other benefits as possible. Possible means promising, not settled. The performance evidence is the strongest part of the case.[1]
