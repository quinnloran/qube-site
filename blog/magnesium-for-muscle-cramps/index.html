Magnesium for muscle cramps: what athletes should know

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Oct 24, 2026Updated Oct 24, 2026Hydration6 minute read

Key takeaways

  • A 2020 Cochrane review of 11 trials and 735 people found no clear benefit of magnesium for cramps compared with placebo.
  • That review found no randomized trials of magnesium for cramps caused by exercise.
  • A sports medicine review found weak support for dehydration and electrolyte loss as the cause of exercise cramps.
  • Magnesium still matters for normal muscle function. It is just not a proven cramp fix.

Every athlete has a cramp story, and almost every athlete has been told to take magnesium for it. I wanted to know if the research backs that up. Short version: magnesium for muscle cramps is a popular idea with surprisingly little evidence behind it, and I would rather say that plainly than sell you on it.

What are exercise-associated muscle cramps?

Exercise-associated muscle cramps are painful, involuntary muscle contractions that happen during or right after training or competition. They are among the most common reasons athletes seek medical care at sporting events, yet their cause is still poorly understood.[2]

That last part matters. When nobody is sure what causes cramps, a simple fix like one mineral is very unlikely to be the full answer.

Is magnesium for muscle cramps backed by research?

Not well. A 2020 Cochrane review pooled 11 randomized trials with 735 people and found magnesium did not clearly reduce cramp frequency, intensity or duration compared with placebo. The authors concluded it is unlikely to give clinically meaningful cramp prevention in older adults.[1]

Here is who was actually studied, because it changes how far you can stretch the result:

  • Five trials looked at leg cramps in pregnancy, and their results conflicted.
  • Five looked at cramps with no identifiable cause, mostly in older adults.
  • One small study looked at people with liver cirrhosis.

Notice who is missing. The Cochrane authors found no randomized trials of exercise-associated cramps and no reliable data for them.[1],[3] So the honest read is two-part: the best available review does not support magnesium for general cramps, and the athlete question has not been tested properly.

One more detail from the commentary on that review: in the trials, diarrhea was reported by roughly 11% to 37% of people taking magnesium by mouth, at doses of 100 to 520 mg of elemental magnesium a day.[3] That is a reason not to treat more magnesium as automatically better.

What actually causes cramps in athletes?

Nobody has proven it. A sports medicine review found the support for dehydration and electrolyte depletion as causes comes mainly from anecdotes, 18 case reports and one small study of 10 people. It called altered neuromuscular control the most likely main cause, with more research needed.[2]

The same review noted that four prospective cohort studies did not support the electrolyte and dehydration explanations. In plain terms, the old idea that cramps mean you are low on a mineral is weaker than most of us were taught. That is a 2009 review, so treat it as one expert reading of the evidence, not the final word.

Does magnesium still matter for athletes?

Yes, just not as a proven cramp remedy. A 2017 review describes magnesium as a cofactor in energy metabolism and reports that the body's need for it increases with physical activity.[4] Getting enough is sensible. Expecting it to end cramps is where the evidence runs out.

The same review lists the Recommended Dietary Allowance at 400 to 420 mg a day for men and 310 to 320 mg for women.[4] If cramps are frequent, severe or new, or if you take any medication, check with a doctor or athletic trainer instead of guessing with supplements.

FAQ

Does magnesium prevent muscle cramps in athletes?

No trial has shown that. The 2020 Cochrane review found no randomized trials of magnesium for exercise-associated cramps, and for other cramps the differences from placebo were small and not statistically significant. The evidence does not support magnesium as a cramp fix.

What causes muscle cramps during exercise?

The cause is not settled. A sports medicine review found weak support for dehydration and electrolyte depletion, and called altered neuromuscular control the most likely main cause, while noting that more evidence is needed.

Is magnesium still useful for athletes?

Magnesium takes part in energy metabolism and normal muscle function, and a 2017 review reports that the need for it rises with physical activity. That is a reason to get enough, not evidence that it stops cramps.

Does QUBE stop muscle cramps?

When should I talk to a doctor about cramps?
