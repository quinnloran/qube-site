When to Take Creatine: Does Timing Really Matter?

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Oct 22, 2026Updated Oct 22, 2026Ingredients5 minute read

  • Muscle creatine builds up over time, so a steady daily habit is the part with the most support.

Teammates ask me when to take creatine more than almost anything else: before the workout, after, or whenever? Here is what the research actually says, including the parts nobody has nailed down.

What is the best time to take creatine?

Researchers have not agreed on a best time to take creatine. A 2021 narrative review found that early evidence leans toward after exercise, but the studies are small and limited, and it concluded that timing around training "should not be considered a real concern for now."[1]

That review looked at about five studies on timing, in bodybuilders and in older adults, and called the findings contradictory. The differences came down to protocols, populations and training programs. In plain terms, the clock matters a lot less than most supplement ads suggest.

Is creatine better before or after a workout?

Maybe slightly better after, but it is not proven. In a 4-week trial, 19 recreational male bodybuilders took 5 g of creatine monohydrate immediately before or immediately after training, and the main statistical tests found no significant difference between the two groups.[2]

The authors used a different statistical approach (magnitude-based inference) and concluded post-workout timing was possibly more beneficial. The 2021 review noted that the confidence intervals overlapped. With 19 people, treat this as a hint, not an answer. Also, these were recreational bodybuilders, so the result may not carry over to a college basketball player's schedule.[1][2]

Why does a daily habit matter more than the clock?

Creatine works by raising the creatine stored in muscle, and that takes days to weeks of regular intake. The 2021 review describes about 28 days at 3 to 5 g a day, or 5 to 7 days at 20 g a day, to saturate stores, and says timing is likely irrelevant once they are full.[1]

Those are the studied amounts, not a recommendation, and a 20 g loading dose is not what QUBE uses. The ISSN position stand adds that short and long-term creatine supplementation, studied at up to 30 g a day for 5 years, was safe and well tolerated in healthy people.[3] If you have a health condition or take medication, check with a doctor or athletic trainer before starting. For the bigger picture on what creatine does, see our post on creatine for athletes.

Should you take creatine on rest days?

The research does not give a firm answer. Several timing studies dosed creatine only on training days, and the 2021 review says the effect of that approach on muscle creatine is unknown. Taking it every day is the simplest way to keep the habit steady.[1]

The habit is what the research points to, not a single dose around a single workout. The same plain-English approach applies to the performance pair. You can read how it works with beta-alanine in creatine and beta-alanine together, or browse every ingredient on the Research page.

FAQ

Is it better to take creatine before or after a workout?

The evidence is not settled. A small 4-week trial found no significant difference between taking 5 g before or after training, and a 2021 review said adapting timing to training sessions should not be considered a real concern for now.

Can you take creatine in the morning?

Yes. The studies reviewed did not identify a best time of day, so a consistent daily habit, such as part of a morning routine, is a reasonable approach.

Should you take creatine on rest days?

The 2021 review notes that the effect of dosing only on training days on muscle creatine is unknown, so taking it daily is the simpler approach to keeping a steady habit.

How long does it take for creatine to build up in muscle?

The 2021 review describes about 28 days at 3 to 5 g a day, or 5 to 7 days at a 20 g a day loading dose. Those are the studied amounts, not a recommendation, and QUBE does not use a loading phase.
