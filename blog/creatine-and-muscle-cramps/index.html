Creatine and muscle cramps: what research shows

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Nov 24, 2026Updated Nov 24, 2026Ingredients6 minute read

Key takeaways

  • A 2009 systematic review found no evidence that creatine hinders heat dissipation or fluid balance, and controlled trials of athletes exercising in the heat found no adverse effects at recommended dosages.[1]
  • A 2003 open-label study reported significantly less cramping in college football players who chose creatine, but it was not randomized.[2]
  • In a 2006 double-blind crossover, 12 dehydrated men taking 21.6 g of creatine a day showed no increase in heat-illness symptoms.[3]
  • These studies used higher amounts than a 5 g daily serving. The ISSN describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintenance.[4]

Somebody on every team swears creatine gives you cramps. I went looking for the actual evidence on creatine and muscle cramps, because I take it and I play in warm gyms. The research points the other way, with real limits I will lay out below. Three studies carry most of the weight.

Does creatine cause muscle cramps?

The research I read does not show that it does. A 2009 systematic review found no evidence that creatine hinders the body's ability to dissipate heat or harms fluid balance, and controlled trials of athletes exercising in the heat found no adverse effects. Cramps were not that review's main outcome, so it speaks to cramp triggers indirectly.[1]

That caveat matters, so the next two studies look at cramps and heat more directly.

What did the 2009 review of creatine and heat find?

Researchers screened 95 articles, found 15 on creatine with thermoregulation or hydration, and selected 10 controlled studies. Those studies used 20 to 25 g of creatine a day for 5 to 28 days. The review concluded that no evidence supports creatine hurting heat tolerance or hydration.[1]

In a pooled analysis of 8 of those studies (167 people), the difference in core body temperature was about 0.03°C, a tiny gap with a confidence interval that included zero.[1]

What did the football study find about creatine and cramping?

In a 2003 study of one NCAA Division I football season, 38 of 72 players chose to take creatine. The creatine group reported significantly less cramping, heat illness or dehydration, muscle tightness and muscle strains than non-users, in conditions up to 37°C.[2]

The design is the catch. It was open-label and the players picked their own group, so other differences between the groups could explain some of the result. I read it as evidence that creatine did not add cramping in real college practices, not as proof that creatine prevents it.[2]

Does creatine make exercise in the heat harder?

In one double-blind crossover, 12 active men took 21.6 g of creatine or placebo for 7 days, dropped 2% of body mass, then did an 80 minute heat test at 33.5°C. Rectal temperature was 39.3 vs 39.4°C (placebo vs creatine), and symptom scores were similar.[3]

What do these creatine cramp studies not tell us?

They do not tell us what causes cramps, and they were short. The heat trials ran 5 to 28 days at 20 to 25 g a day, far more than a daily serving, and the review itself says future research should look at longer supplementation periods.[1]

Nothing here was tested on QUBE. If you cramp often, creatine is not proven innocent for every person, so loop in a doctor or athletic trainer rather than guessing.

What should athletes do about cramps if they take creatine?

Keep the creatine habit steady, because the ISSN describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintenance, and look at the bigger picture of fluids, sleep and workload with your trainer.[4] I covered the broader hydration question in does creatine dehydrate you, plus electrolytes for athletes and magnesium for muscle cramps.

FAQ

Does creatine cause muscle cramps?

The research I read does not show that it does. A 2009 systematic review found no evidence that creatine hinders heat dissipation or fluid balance, and a 2003 study of college football players reported less cramping in the group that took creatine. That study was not randomized, so it is a signal, not proof.

Does creatine make you cramp in the heat?

Not in the controlled trials reviewed in 2009. In a 2006 double-blind crossover, 12 dehydrated men took 21.6 g of creatine a day for 7 days before an 80 minute heat test. Heat-illness symptom scores were similar to placebo.

Do I need to drink extra water if I take creatine?

The studies did not find a hydration penalty, so there is no research-based reason to change your habits for creatine alone. Drinking enough fluid around hard training and heat still matters for every athlete, and your athletic trainer can tailor that for you.

How much creatine did the cramp and heat studies use?

The heat studies in the 2009 review used 20 to 25 g a day for 5 to 28 days, and the 2003 football study used a short loading phase then a smaller daily amount. The ISSN describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintenance, which is closer to a daily serving like QUBE's 5 g.

What should I do if I keep getting cramps?

Talk with a doctor or athletic trainer. Cramps have many possible triggers, and these studies cannot tell you which one applies to you. Do not assume creatine is the cause or that stopping it will fix the problem.
