Creatine and caffeine: do they cancel out?

By Quinn, Founder of QUBE

Published Nov 12, 2026Updated Nov 12, 2026Ingredients4 minute read

Key takeaways

  • An older idea says caffeine blunts creatine, but a 2016 randomized trial of 54 men found no performance penalty from adding about 300 mg of caffeine a day.[2]
  • A 2015 review calls a pharmacokinetic interaction unlikely, and says limited research suggests caffeine may reduce creatine's benefits.[1]
  • The one clear signal was mild stomach discomfort in 4 of 13 people taking creatine with caffeine pills.[2]
  • The practical move is consistency: the ISSN describes 3 to 5 g of creatine a day for maintenance.[3]

Half my team drinks coffee before anything else, and half of us take creatine. So the question comes up constantly: creatine and caffeine, does one cancel the other? I read the research. The honest answer is "probably not, but the evidence is thin and a little mixed."

Does caffeine cancel out creatine?

Probably not. A 2016 randomized trial gave 54 active men creatine loading alone or with caffeine or coffee, and found no performance difference between groups. A 2015 review, though, says limited research suggests caffeine may reduce creatine's benefits.[1][2]

That split is the whole story. The worry has a history, but the more recent trial did not back it up.

What did the 2015 review conclude about creatine and caffeine?

The review found that creatine reliably improves strength and power in short, intense efforts, while caffeine's effects on strength and sprinting are inconsistent. Because the two work through separate mechanisms, they are often combined in products, yet limited research suggests caffeine may reduce creatine's benefits.[1]

The authors proposed possible reasons, including effects on muscle relaxation time or stomach upset, and said a pharmacokinetic interaction (caffeine changing how creatine is absorbed or processed) seems unlikely.[1]

What did the 2016 trial of creatine and caffeine find?

Researchers split 54 physically active men into four groups for 5 days: creatine alone, creatine plus caffeine anhydrous, creatine plus coffee, or placebo. The caffeine groups got about 300 mg a day. Adding caffeine or coffee did not significantly change strength or sprint results.[2]

One caveat matters. The authors concluded that creatine alone did not significantly beat placebo in this short 5-day window, so the trial could not show caffeine blocking a benefit that never showed up. It tells us caffeine did not make things worse in this setup, not that creatine's full effect was tested.[2]

Can creatine and caffeine upset your stomach?

Possibly. In the 2016 trial, 4 participants in the creatine plus caffeine anhydrous group (of 13) reported mild gastrointestinal discomfort. No side effects were reported in the other groups, including the coffee group.[2]

That is a small sample, so treat it as a reason to pay attention to how you feel, not as a rule.

How should athletes take creatine and caffeine in practice?

Take creatine consistently. The ISSN position stand describes 3 to 5 g a day for maintenance, and creatine builds up in muscle over time, so the daily habit matters more than the exact minute.[3] If you also drink coffee, the available research does not tell you to give it up.

If you ever feel off, check with a doctor or athletic trainer. For more on the timing question, read when to take creatine, and for how fast stores fill, creatine loading.

FAQ

Does caffeine cancel out creatine?

Not in the best recent data. A 2016 randomized trial of 54 men found creatine plus caffeine or coffee did not change strength or sprint results compared with creatine alone. An older study suggested caffeine might blunt creatine, so the evidence is mixed.

Can I take creatine with coffee?

The 2016 trial gave about 300 mg of caffeine a day, from caffeine pills or coffee, alongside creatine and found no performance penalty. Four of 13 people taking creatine with caffeine pills reported mild stomach discomfort, so see how you feel.

Does caffeine affect how creatine is absorbed?

A 2015 review says a pharmacokinetic interaction, meaning caffeine changing how creatine is absorbed or processed, seems unlikely. The authors raised other possible explanations for any blunting, such as effects on muscle relaxation time or stomach upset.

Should I take creatine and caffeine at the same time?

The studies do not show timing matters for this question. Creatine works through a daily dose that builds up in muscle over time, so the clock matters less than taking it consistently (3 to 5 g a day per the ISSN).
